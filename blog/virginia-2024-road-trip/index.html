Our 2024 Virginia Road Trip: The Coast, Roanoke and Walton's Mountain

November 2024

Asia and I were on Virginia Beach at sunrise when we saw a proposal out on the sand. The sun was coming up over the water, and a couple was out there on the beach. Asia filmed it, and she was able to share her video with the couple afterward. It moved her, and of everything on this trip, that's the moment she loved most.

In November 2024, we flew from Dallas to Norfolk. We started on the Virginia coast, then drove west, with a stop at New River Gorge in West Virginia, and through some of Virginia's history, including Walton's Mountain in Schuyler.

Here's how the trip went, in the order we drove it.

Norfolk and Virginia Beach

We landed in Norfolk and stayed at Hilton Norfolk The Main, a modern 23-story hotel at the corner of Main and Granby Streets, right on the downtown waterfront, overlooking the Elizabeth River. From our room we looked out over the river and the city skyline. In the evening the lights came on across the water, and that same view was waiting when we came back to the window later. The staff were warm and welcoming from the moment we checked in.

Our first dinner was at Rockafeller's in Virginia Beach, looking out over Rudee Inlet. Asia had oysters with mignonette, and I had pork ribs. The next morning we were back on the beach for sunrise, and that's when we saw the proposal. Afterward we sat down to breakfast at Pocahontas Pancake & Waffle House.

Cape Charles and the Chrysler Museum

From there we crossed the Chesapeake Bay Bridge-Tunnel to Cape Charles, a long run of bridges and tunnels with the water on either side. We walked around the historic downtown and along the waterfront, and we ate at The Oyster Farm. Asia had oysters again, and I had a cheeseburger that was one of the best I've ever had. We watched sailboats out on the bay while we were there.

Back in Norfolk, we went to the Chrysler Museum of Art. Admission was free. We spent time inside among the galleries, and Asia fell in love with the glass collection, including work by Chihuly and Tiffany.

This part of the trip is in our film Norfolk, Virginia Beach & Cape Charles.

Suffolk and Appomattox

From the coast we headed west and stopped in Suffolk, where we had some peanut snacks and spent a little time downtown. Then we went on to Appomattox Court House National Historical Park. The park is a cluster of old buildings set among open fields, and we saw the McLean House there.

Roanoke and New River Gorge

In Roanoke we stayed at The Hotel Roanoke & Conference Center, a Tudor-style hotel near downtown, in Virginia's Blue Ridge. It was built in 1882 as the railroad hotel at the heart of the growing town, and it has been taking in guests for more than a century. It's on the National Register of Historic Places, and it's part of Hilton's Curio Collection. The hotel closed in 1989 and reopened in 1995 after a restoration. The distinctive Tudor exterior is hard to miss. Inside, you come first into a lobby filled with antiques, where the original Czech-made chandeliers still hang, along with the woodwork and the old elevator doors. The staff here were warm and welcoming too, and that made the old place feel like home.

We also crossed into West Virginia to visit New River Gorge National Park, including the New River Gorge Bridge. From the overlooks we looked down at the river and out at the long steel arch spanning the gorge.

Back in Roanoke, we visited the Basilica of Saint Andrew, with its stained glass, and the Norfolk & Western Historical Society, where we looked at the exhibits, including a train model. Then we went up to the Roanoke Star, on the mountain above town, with the city spread out below and the star standing among the trees.

This part of the trip is in our film Appomattox, Roanoke & New River Gorge.

Schuyler, Monticello and Lynchburg

We visited Schuyler, the hometown of Earl Hamner Jr., who created The Waltons, and the real-life inspiration for Walton's Mountain. We went to Walton's Mountain Museum and saw his printing press, and we also visited the Walton Hamner House.

In Lynchburg we stayed at The Virginian Hotel, on Church Street downtown. It opened in 1913 as a five-story Beaux-Arts hotel, designed by Verus T. Ritter, and after a complete renovation it reopened in 2018 as part of Hilton's Curio Collection. It's a historic, restored landmark, and you see that as soon as you come in: arched windows, an ornate lobby with 30-foot barrel-vaulted ceilings and curved skylights, and grand marble stairs with wrought iron railings. After dark, we looked out from the hotel over the street and the buildings downtown. And like the other two hotels on this trip, the staff were warm and welcoming.

From there we went to Thomas Jefferson's Poplar Forest, and then to Lexington, where we visited Lee Chapel, walked the grounds at Washington & Lee University, and passed under the arch at Virginia Military Institute. We also went to Monticello and to Michie Tavern, which dates to around 1784, and we ate there. We visited James Monroe's Highland, walked around Lynchburg, and stopped at Liberty University, including DeMoss Hall and the School of Business.

This part of the trip is in our film From The Waltons to Monticello.
