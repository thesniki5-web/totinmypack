Route 66 on the Way to Kansas City

January 2013 and January 2023

Some trips are about the destination. These were supposed to be. Asia and I have driven from Texas to Kansas City twice, in January 2013 and again in January 2023, and somewhere along the way we decided the fastest road wasn't the most interesting one. So we got off the highway and onto old Route 66, and I'm really glad we did.

Route 66 turned 100 this year, and you can feel that age in the best way. The towns are smaller, the signs are hand-painted, and every few miles somebody has built something big and a little crazy just to make you pull over. We pulled over a lot.

Here are the stops from those drives, in the order we made them.

The Golden Driller

Our first big stop was the Golden Driller in Tulsa, Oklahoma. He's a giant statue of an oil worker, and he looks every bit the part of Oklahoma's oil country. We took photos with Asia in front of him, and he really is huge.

The Blue Whale of Catoosa

Next came the Blue Whale of Catoosa. It's a big, smiling blue whale sitting at the edge of a pond, right off Route 66. We walked inside and all around it, and we took plenty of photos.

Ed Galloway's Totem Pole Park

After the whale we found the totem poles at Ed Galloway's Totem Pole Park, just off Route 66 in Foyil, Oklahoma. These are tall, brightly painted concrete totems, hand-built a long time ago and still standing out there in the Oklahoma countryside.

Baxter Springs

Crossing into Kansas, our first stop was Baxter Springs, a small Route 66 town. We stopped at the Route 66 visitor information center there and took more photos.

Rainbow Bridge

Just north of Baxter Springs, we stopped at the Rainbow Bridge. It's an old white arched bridge from the early days of the highway, and it's a well-known stop on this stretch of Route 66. Kansas only has a short piece of the old road, about 13 miles, but it makes every mile count. We walked out on it and took a photo there too.

The OKKAMO Tri-State Marker

One of the stops was the OKKAMO Tri-State Marker near Joplin, where Oklahoma, Kansas and Missouri meet. It's close to Route 66, and you can stand in all three states at once.

Just Driving the Road

In between all the named stops was the best part, which was just driving it. Old Route 66 winds through small towns, past old gas stations and faded signs, and it gives you time to look around instead of just getting somewhere. If you're making a drive like this, give yourself a few extra hours. You'll want them.

Kansas City: The Majestic and Hotel Phillips

When we finally rolled into Kansas City, we ate at the Majestic, and I had the steak. The piano player was amazing.

We stayed at Hotel Phillips Kansas City, part of Hilton's Curio Collection. We love the art deco style, and the rooms are great. It's our go-to place to stay in Kansas City.

If you've driven Route 66, I'd love to hear your favorite stop. And if you haven't, take the old road next time. Pull over for the whale. You won't regret it.
